# High Hemoglobin / High RBC Count (Polycythemia)
> AI-drafted, pending medical review. Educational only — not a medical diagnosis.

**Marker:** Hemoglobin is the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells, and the RBC count measures how many red cells are present. These values usually move together. When they are above the normal range, the general term is polycythemia (sometimes called erythrocytosis) — more red cells or hemoglobin than usual.

**What an abnormal result may relate to:** A higher-than-normal hemoglobin or RBC count can mean the blood is more concentrated with red cells. Often this reflects something straightforward, such as dehydration making the blood appear more concentrated, or the body making extra red cells in response to lower oxygen availability. Less commonly, it can relate to a bone-marrow process that produces too many red cells. Because the everyday causes are so common, results are usually interpreted alongside hydration status, lifestyle, and the rest of the blood count.

**Common contributing factors:**
- **Dehydration** — reduced fluid makes red cells more concentrated, raising the apparent count (a "relative" rise rather than truly more cells).
- **Living at high altitude** — lower oxygen prompts the body to make more red cells over time.
- **Smoking** — associated with higher red-cell values in many people.
- **Chronic low oxygen** — some heart or lung conditions can drive increased red-cell production.
- **Bone-marrow-related conditions** — less common, considered when values are markedly or persistently high.

**Commonly associated symptoms:**
- Often none, especially with mild elevations or dehydration-related rises.
- With more pronounced elevations, some people report headaches, flushing, dizziness, or a feeling of fullness.

**General (non-prescriptive) lifestyle context:**
- Staying well hydrated is commonly mentioned, since dehydration is a frequent reason for a falsely high-looking result.
- Not smoking is frequently noted in this context.
- Because a genuinely high red-cell count can have several causes, understanding the reason matters more than any single lifestyle change.

**How concerned to be:** A mildly high hemoglobin or RBC count, particularly when dehydration is likely, is common and often resolves with rehydration and a recheck. It becomes more meaningful when values are markedly high, persistent, or accompanied by symptoms such as headaches, dizziness, or clotting concerns — situations generally considered reasons for medical evaluation. As a general pattern, a modest elevation is often rechecked after ensuring good hydration.

**Important:** This information is educational only and not a medical diagnosis. Please consult a licensed doctor to interpret your report and decide on any next steps.
