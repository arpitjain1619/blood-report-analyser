# Abnormal T3 / T4 (Thyroid Hormone Levels)
> AI-drafted, pending medical review. Educational only — not a medical diagnosis.

**Marker:** T4 (thyroxine) and T3 (triiodothyronine) are the actual hormones the thyroid produces to regulate the body's metabolism — how it uses energy. They are reported as "free" (the active, unbound portion) and/or "total" forms. While TSH is the pituitary's *signal* to the thyroid, T3 and T4 are the *output*, so they are usually read together with TSH to build a complete picture.

**What an abnormal result may relate to:** Because TSH and thyroid hormones work as a feedback loop, they are interpreted as a set. Broadly: high thyroid hormones with a low TSH point toward an overactive thyroid, while low thyroid hormones with a high TSH point toward an underactive one. Sometimes the pattern is mixed or subtle, which is exactly why T3/T4 are measured alongside TSH rather than relying on any single value. On their own, an abnormal T3 or T4 is a piece of the puzzle rather than a conclusion.

**Common contributing factors:**
- The same conditions behind abnormal TSH — autoimmune thyroid conditions, nodules, inflammation, iodine imbalance, and certain medications.
- **Non-thyroid illness** — significant illness unrelated to the thyroid can temporarily shift these hormone levels, a well-recognized pattern that a doctor accounts for.
- **Binding-protein changes** — pregnancy and some medications can alter *total* T3/T4 without the active free levels being truly abnormal, which is why free levels are often preferred.

**Commonly associated symptoms:**
- Symptoms track with the overall thyroid state: an overactive pattern (high hormones) tends toward weight loss, rapid heartbeat, and feeling hot; an underactive pattern (low hormones) tends toward fatigue, weight gain, and feeling cold.
- Abnormal T3/T4 without a clear overall pattern may cause no specific symptoms.

**General (non-prescriptive) lifestyle context:**
- As with the rest of the thyroid panel, this is a medical matter rather than a lifestyle-corrected one.
- The practical point is that these values are meaningful mainly in combination with TSH, so interpretation belongs with a doctor.

**How concerned to be:** An isolated, mildly abnormal T3 or T4 — especially during or after another illness — is often rechecked rather than acted on, since it can shift for non-thyroid reasons. It carries more weight when it fits a clear pattern with TSH and with symptoms. As a general pattern, thyroid hormones are interpreted as part of the whole panel, and a clear, symptomatic pattern is what prompts prompter follow-up.

**Important:** This information is educational only and not a medical diagnosis. Please consult a licensed doctor to interpret your report and decide on any next steps.
