# High LDL Cholesterol
> AI-drafted, pending medical review. Educational only — not a medical diagnosis.

**Marker:** LDL (low-density lipoprotein) carries cholesterol through the bloodstream to the body's tissues. It is often nicknamed "bad" cholesterol because, when levels are high, cholesterol is more likely to be deposited in the walls of blood vessels over time. LDL is usually reported as part of a lipid profile alongside total cholesterol, HDL, and triglycerides.

**What an abnormal result may relate to:** Higher-than-desirable LDL is associated with a greater long-term risk of fatty buildup (plaque) in arteries, which relates to cardiovascular health over years rather than causing immediate symptoms. LDL is typically described in tiers (from optimal upward), and where a given number falls on that scale — together with a person's other risk factors — shapes how much attention it gets. It is very much a "big picture, long-term" marker.

**Common contributing factors:**
- **Diet high in saturated and trans fats** — a well-known contributor.
- **Low physical activity.**
- **Excess body weight.**
- **Genetics** — for some people, high LDL is strongly inherited (familial), sometimes producing high levels despite a healthy lifestyle.
- **Other factors** — certain medical conditions and medications can raise LDL.

**Commonly associated symptoms:**
- High LDL itself causes no symptoms — this is why it is sometimes called a "silent" risk factor and is found only through testing.
- Any consequences relate to long-term cardiovascular effects rather than day-to-day feelings.

**General (non-prescriptive) lifestyle context:**
- Dietary patterns lower in saturated fat and higher in fiber (vegetables, whole grains, legumes) are commonly discussed.
- Regular physical activity and maintaining a healthy weight are frequently mentioned.
- Because genetics play a large role for some people, lifestyle changes are not always enough on their own — which is a discussion to have with a doctor rather than a reason for self-treatment.

**How concerned to be:** High LDL is generally a long-term risk consideration rather than an urgent finding, and mildly elevated levels are common and often addressed with lifestyle changes over time. It carries more weight when it is substantially elevated, or when it appears alongside other cardiovascular risk factors (such as high blood pressure, smoking, diabetes, or a strong family history) — combinations a doctor weighs together. As a general pattern, LDL is acted on based on overall risk, not the number alone.

**Important:** This information is educational only and not a medical diagnosis. Please consult a licensed doctor to interpret your report and decide on any next steps.
